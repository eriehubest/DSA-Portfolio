# DSA Supporting Write-Up

This document is meant to accompany the website and serve as a first introduction before an examiner decides whether to explore the portfolio in more detail.

## Main Version

I am applying through DSA because mathematics and computing have become the two main ways I engage seriously with difficult problems. In mathematics, that interest developed into a sustained Olympiad and competition record across UKMT and AMC, where I learned to look for structure, build precise arguments, and stay accurate under pressure. What drew me in was not only the challenge of solving problems, but the discipline of making each step of an argument hold together cleanly.

In computing, my path has been largely self-taught and independent. Rather than learning through small isolated exercises, I taught myself by building projects from scratch and gradually taking on more technical complexity. My main work is the portfolio website and its linked project gallery, both of which I designed and developed independently. More important than the tools themselves was the way the work forced me to think: how different systems should be separated, how interactions between components should be controlled, and how a project can remain understandable as it grows more complex.

The deeper technical project in the gallery is a modular vehicle systems project. In that work, I organised rendering, physics, input handling, camera logic, world construction, resource loading, and timing into separate but connected systems. The central challenge was not implementing each feature individually, but designing the relationships between them so that the project remained stable, maintainable, and coherent. Building it taught me to think less in terms of isolated features and more in terms of architecture, update flow, and how changes in one part of a system affect the rest.

What connects mathematics and computing most strongly for me is structure. In mathematics, I am drawn to problems where a hidden pattern or invariant gradually becomes visible through careful reasoning. In computing, I am drawn to systems where complexity can be reduced by understanding how components relate to one another. In both areas, I find the same satisfaction in making something difficult become legible and internally consistent.

Through DSA, I hope to join an environment that will push me towards problems requiring abstraction, sustained thought, and independent construction. I am especially looking for opportunities where rigorous reasoning and technical depth are both necessary, and where I can continue developing the habit of turning complex ideas into clear and well-structured work.
